### Content Gaps

_These are things a cautious first-time buyer would want to know that are missing from the current deal page._

1. No mention of total course hours or estimated completion time — customer cannot assess the time commitment required to complete certification
2. Accreditation claim ('Expert Skills Accredited' badge) is not explained — no information on which accrediting body provides certification or whether it is recognized by hospitals/employers in hiring
3. No description of what certification customers receive upon completion (certificate format, issuing body, credentials earned) — critical for employability and resume value
4. Course delivery format not specified (video lectures, reading materials, interactive simulations, live sessions) — customer doesn't know the actual learning experience
5. Original prices not shown despite 'Up to 50% Off' claim in title — customer cannot verify the discount or compare value to market alternatives
6. Bundle claims 'Save $60 versus buying separately' but individual courses are $29.99 each ($59.98 total) vs. bundle at $59.99 — the math shows <$1 savings, not $60, and this discrepancy is unexplained
7. Fine print states 'Consultation required before service; if you are ineligible, a refund will be provided' with no explanation of what makes someone ineligible for an online course or what the consultation entails
8. No instructor credentials, qualifications, or healthcare background shown — important for credibility in medical/clinical education
9. No information on technical requirements (compatible devices, browsers, internet speed, software) needed to access and complete the online course

### ⚠️ AI Flags

- Bundle savings claim appears mathematically incorrect: page states 'Save $60 versus buying separately' but individual courses are $29.99 each (total $59.98) while the bundle is $59.99 — this represents ~$0 savings, not $60. This misleading claim could seriously damage trust and may violate advertising standards.
- Title claims 'Up to 50% Off' but no original prices are shown anywhere on the page — the discount percentage is completely unverifiable by the customer
- 'Expert Skills Accredited' badge is prominently displayed throughout but there is zero explanation of what body provides this accreditation, whether it's industry-recognized, or what standards it represents — this is an unsubstantiated credential claim
- Review quotes are extremely generic ('Great communication and excellent quality', 'Fast, reliable, and professional') and don't reference online courses, learning outcomes, or certification value — raises questions about review relevance or authenticity for this product type
- Stark mismatch between 3,425 reviews (very high) and only '11 bought' (extremely low sold count) — this unusual ratio may confuse customers or suggest reviews are from different products/time periods
